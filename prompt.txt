## Scientific and engineering requirements

- Start with a simple green-channel baseline, then consider CHROM or POS after the baseline works.
- Do not treat estimated heart rate alone as proof that a video is real or fake.
- Do not assume real facial regions must always agree or fake regions must always disagree. Test whether the features actually distinguish the available data.
- Do not hard-code the expected heart rate or classifier outcome.
- Use realistic validation and report actual measured results only.
- Split data by subject and source video before training. Keep derived clips and real/fake pairs from the same source on the same side of the split.
- Avoid claiming that performance generalizes to unseen datasets unless it has been tested on them.
- Account for lighting, motion, compression, skin tone, frame rate, clip duration, and video quality.
- Treat this as an experimental research prototype, not a production-grade forensic authentication system.
